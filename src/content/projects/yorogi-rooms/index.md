      "text": "Located in Fukuoka, Japan, the project starts from the observation of the river edge near Ōhashi, where the pedestrian and cycling path runs alongside the Naka River without creating a strong relationship with the water. The café acts as a device to redirect this linear movement toward the river. Rather than standing as an isolated object, the volume extends from the embankment, turning the existing path into a sequence of deviation, approach and pause. Its main gesture is a projection toward the water: the volume stretches outward and inclines, lowering the gaze and guiding the body toward the river surface."

      "text": "The project is organized around different users and rhythms along the riverfront, shaping the café into three spatial conditions. Move is the fastest, linked to pedestrian and cycling flows, enabling quick take-away and brief interaction. Pause introduces a slower rhythm of waiting and detachment from movement. Still is the most intimate condition, where sitting close to the water transforms the river into a physical and perceptive presence. This final condition draws from a childhood memory of watching flowing water, translated into a spatial experience of reflection, intimacy and stillness rather than a literal representation."
