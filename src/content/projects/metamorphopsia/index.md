      "type": "text",
      "text": "Metamorphopsia, from the Greek “vision of distorted forms,” refers to a condition where straight lines appear wavy or fragmented, altering perception. This notion of distortion was used as the conceptual foundation of the project, both as a metaphor for unstable human perception and as a reflection on the fragmented condition of contemporary life. The installation translated this idea into space through a sequence of ambiguous thresholds, semi-transparent fabrics, and diffused light that guided visitors along uncertain paths. The experience culminated in a luminous cylinder, invisible from outside, where light and reflections produced shifting colors and mirrored fragments, immersing the visitor in a disorienting atmosphere that questioned the reliability of reality itself."

      "text": "The project was communicated through a book designed not only as documentation but as a continuation of the installation’s experiential qualities. Great attention was given to materiality: metal foils and reflective surfaces were included to reproduce the sensation of distortion, while fabrics echoed the spatial qualities of the temporary installation. The book also retraced the entire design process, from early sketches to conceptual diagrams and intermediate tests, making visible the progressive steps that led to the final vision. By combining graphic content with physical, tactile elements, the book itself became a sensory object."
