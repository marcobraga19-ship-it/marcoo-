      "text": "Located between Via Mentana, Via Procaccini and Via Buonarroti, the former municipal slaughterhouse of Monza is a 44,000 m² industrial complex inaugurated in 1906 and progressively abandoned after its activity ended in the early 1980s. Originally organised into the slaughterhouse, livestock market and fruit-and-vegetable market, the site now survives as a fragmented and largely unused urban remainder. ODD — Officine del Dialogo approaches this “avanzo” not as an empty area to replace, but as an existing material and social resource, reactivating its gallery, stable and open spaces through intercultural exchange, play and conviviality."

      "type": "text",
      "text": "The project concept draws on Social Penetration Theory, which describes interpersonal relationships as a gradual process of mutual knowledge. Identity is understood as a sequence of layers: the outer ones are immediately visible, while memories, values and cultural backgrounds emerge only through time, trust and interaction. This idea is expressed through the volti sommersi, literally “submerged faces.” The visible portion represents what is perceived during a first encounter, while the rest remains hidden below the waterline. Water also recalls migration routes: it is simultaneously a boundary, a passage and a space of memory for many migrants, the project’s main users. Dialogue becomes the process through which hidden identities and stories progressively emerge."
    },
    {
      "type": "text",
      "text": "Inaugurated in 1906, the former slaughterhouse supported Monza’s municipal food infrastructure before closing in the early 1980s. The site was studied through an objective analysis of dimensions, accessibility, structures, vegetation and conservation, together with a subjective reading of silence, material traces, light and abandonment. These complementary approaches revealed both its original functioning and present atmosphere. Within the wider project, our group focused on Area A. The Gallery, Stables and green spaces corresponding to the first layers of Social Penetration Theory, where play, informal exchange, culture and conviviality gradually initiate dialogue."
    },
    {
      "type": "text",
      "text": "The Gallery translates the first stage of dialogue into a structured experience. Visitors enter from opposite ends and begin with a symbolic hand-washing ritual that marks the transition into the project. Separate paths, unfamiliar signs and incomplete actions initially create misunderstanding. Through complementary games, meanings gradually become clearer and individual gestures begin to depend on one another. The two routes finally meet at the centre, where cooperation transforms incomprehension into a shared language and experience."
    },

      "type": "text",
      "text": "Beyond the Gallery, dialogue continues through slower and more informal forms of exchange. Existing fences are reused to define new routes between educational gardens, vegetable plots and sensory landscapes, preserving the memory of the site while creating opportunities for encounter and shared activity. These spaces allow visitors to pause, observe and participate in everyday practices. The landscape therefore extends the dialogue initiated indoors, transforming walking, cultivating and gathering into occasions for interaction, cooperation and belonging."
